Training zones — know these cold
Easy (E)
Full conversation · nose breathing
7:20–7:45/mi
130–150 bpm
The majority of all your running. Builds aerobic base, repairs tissue, flushes fatigue. Never push easy runs faster — the benefit is the volume, not the pace. If it feels embarrassingly slow, that's right. Trust it.
Threshold (T)
Short sentences · comfortably hard
6:40–6:50/mi
162–172 bpm
Most important zone for this race. T-pace = lactate threshold — the pace you could hold ~60 min max. Training here raises that ceiling. Notice it's nearly identical to your 6:45 race goal. This is the workout that wins the Peachtree.
Interval (I)
A few words · controlled hard
6:15–6:25/mi
174–185 bpm
VO2max pace. Short 1K reps with full rest between. Not a sprint — focused 3-minute effort. These raise your aerobic ceiling so 6:45 feels like a cruise on race day.
HR zones are a guide, not gospel. Max HR formulas are wrong for trained athletes. Trust feel first — if easy runs feel conversational around 145–150 bpm you're dialed. If 155 still feels easy, trust the feeling.
Before EVERY run · EVERY week · all 5 weeks
Takes 8 minutes. Non-negotiable — especially with low back soreness. Activates hip flexors and glutes before each run. Skip them and your back will remind you why you shouldn't have.
Leg Swings
10 each direction, each leg
Forward/back then side-to-side. Do this one first. Unlocks hip flexors — critical for your low back.
A-March
2 × 20m
Exaggerated high-knee march, pump arms. Activates hip flexors and sets proper knee lift mechanics.
A-Skip
2 × 20m
Same as A-march with a skip rhythm. Adds pop and ground contact timing on top of knee drive.
B-Skip
2 × 20m
High knee then extend and claw foot down. Teaches the pawing motion — most important running mechanic.
Butt Kicks
2 × 20m
Heels to glutes, fast turnover. Trains hamstring recovery speed and high-cadence muscle memory.
Strides
6–8 × 100m when listed
Smooth accelerations to mile effort. Full walk recovery between. Maintain form — not a sprint.
