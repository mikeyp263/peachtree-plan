Training zones — know these cold
Easy (E)
Full conversation · nose breathing
7:20–7:45/mi
130–150 bpm
The majority of all your running. Builds aerobic base, repairs tissue, flushes fatigue. Never push easy runs faster — the benefit is the volume, not the pace. If it feels embarrassingly slow, that's right. Trust it.
Threshold (T)
Short sentences · comfortably hard
6:40–6:50/mi
162–172 bpm
Most important zone for this race. T-pace = lactate threshold — the pace you could hold ~60 min max. Nearly identical to your 6:45 race goal. This is the workout that wins the Peachtree.
Interval (I)
A few words · controlled hard
6:15–6:25/mi
174–185 bpm
VO2max pace. Short 1K reps with full rest. Not a sprint — focused 3-minute effort. Raises your aerobic ceiling so 6:45 feels like a cruise on race day.
Returning runner note: After 2 years off, your HR will run high even at easy effort — ignore the numbers, trust the feel. Week 1 pace may be 8:00–8:30/mi. That's correct. The muscle memory is there; the aerobic machinery needs a few weeks to reopen. Pace comes back faster than you expect.
HR zones are a guide, not gospel. Trained athletes often run at 150+ bpm on easy days due to deconditioning. Trust feel first — full conversation = easy, regardless of HR reading.
Before EVERY run · EVERY week · all 4 weeks
Takes 8 minutes. Non-negotiable with low back pain — your back is compensating for hip flexors and glutes that have been dormant 2 years. Get them firing first or every run loads the wrong thing.
Low back protocol — do this after every run
Hip flexor stretch: Kneeling lunge, back knee down, push hips forward 30 sec each side. The hip flexor pulls on L4/L5 when tight — this is the primary cause of runner's low back pain.

Glute bridge: On your back, feet flat, push hips up and hold 2 sec × 15 reps. Wakes up glutes so they do their job instead of your back doing it.

Cat-cow: On all fours, 10 slow reps. Decompress the spine after running.

Rules: Stop running if you feel sharp or radiating pain. Dull soreness during warmup that fades = OK to continue. Pain that worsens as you run = stop, walk home, mark it short.
Leg Swings
10 each direction, each leg
Forward/back then side-to-side. Do this one first. Unlocks hip flexors — critical for your low back.
A-March
2 × 20m
Exaggerated high-knee march, pump arms. Activates hip flexors and sets proper knee lift mechanics.
A-Skip
2 × 20m
Same as A-march with a skip rhythm. Adds pop and ground contact timing on top of knee drive.
B-Skip
2 × 20m
High knee then extend and claw foot down. Teaches the pawing motion — most important running mechanic.
Butt Kicks
2 × 20m
Heels to glutes, fast turnover. Trains hamstring recovery speed and high-cadence muscle memory.
Strides
6–8 × 100m when listed
Smooth accelerations to mile effort. Full walk recovery. Maintain form — not a sprint.
